Working paper

Policy Leakage and Regulatory Arbitrage in Vehicle Markets (submitted)
with Sebastian Tebbe & Stephanie Weber
Abstract: We study anticipatory avoidance of vehicle regulations. Using Swedish administrative data, we document that households and firms brought forward the adoption of dirtier vehicles in response to the 2018 feebate policy. We identify a new channel for avoidance: more than half of anticipatory adoption was mediated by dealers who pre-registered vehicles to lock in favorable tax treatment, which we term the \textit{dealer arbitrage effect}. Anticipatory responses are larger among those with greater liquidity and those for whom the policy is more costly, while charging access predicts more post-policy switching. We estimate small environmental costs but substantial fiscal costs from anticipation.

Agricultural Burning, Air Pollution and Road Safety: Evidence from Pakistan
Job Market Paper
Abstract: Using administrative emergency-response records from Punjab, Pakistan, from 2022 to 2025, I study the causal effect of air pollution on road accidents. To address the endogeneity of local air pollution, I exploit variation in previous-day non-local upwind agricultural fires as a source of plausibly exogenous variation in local air quality. Preliminary results indicate that a one-standard-deviation increase in aerosol optical depth (AOD) increases the expected number of daily road accidents by approximately 5.57\% during the crop-burning season. A one-standard-deviation increase in previous-day non-local upwind fires raises local AOD by approximately 3.4\%. Ongoing work investigates the mechanisms underlying this effect, distinguishing visibility deterioration and driver impairment from behavioral avoidance. In particular, I use traffic-volume data to examine whether people reduce or postpone travel in response to severe air pollution, potentially offsetting part of the increase in accident risk. I also evaluate temporary road closures during severe smog episodes as a policy response, asking whether the resulting reductions in accident losses are sufficient to offset their mobility and economic costs. More broadly, the paper highlights how pollution generated by agricultural burning can impose downstream road-safety externalities and informs the design of both air-pollution control and transport policies.
The Heterogeneous Environmental Benefits of Electric Vehicles in China
with Leslie Martin
Abstract: This paper quantifies some of the distributional effects of electric vehicle (EV) adoption in China, the largest global EV market. Electric Vehicles (EVs) are vital for decarbonizing transportation; however, their adoption increases emissions from electricity generation in fossil fuel-dependent grids like China’s. Using satellite data, we estimate monthly PM2.5 concentrations attributable to coal-fired power plants and apply an econometric model to calculate marginal emission factors at the provincial-plant level. We then use an air circulation model to measure EV-related pollution in downwind counties. We find that EV adoption in Northeast China significantly increases population exposure to PM2.5 levels, whereas downwind impacts are smaller when EVs are charged in the Central provinces. In Beijing, one of the cities with the greatest concentration of EVs, redistribution of PM2.5 damages from urban to downwind counties predominantly affects populations in lower-income counties. Finally, we provide suggestive evidence that the delayed rollout of market reforms in China’s electricity has contributed to a non-negligeable share of the cost of pollution in the Northeast. Our results highlight the risk that in the shift to EVs disadvantaged peri-urban areas may bear a disproportionate burden of pollution, while residents in more developed cities disproportionally benefit, raising concerns over environmental justice.
